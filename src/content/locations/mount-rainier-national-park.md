# Mount Rainier National Park

From Paradise, the mountain rises to 14,410 feet. It is the highest peak in Washington and the highest volcanic peak in the contiguous United States (NPS, USGS). Stand on the south slope and it fills the sky. The visitor hub sits at about 5,400 feet. The summit is another mile and three-quarters straight up. The stored map point marks Paradise, not the summit.

The mountain carries more glacier ice than any peak in the lower 48 (USGS). About two dozen glaciers spread from its flanks — roughly 25, with counts that vary across sources and keep declining. Emmons Glacier covers the largest area of any glacier in the contiguous U.S.; Carbon Glacier holds the greatest volume (NPS, USGS). The NPS calls this the largest alpine glacial system outside Alaska. The ice is not fixed. Nisqually Glacier holds the record for the fastest measured movement of any glacier here, about 29 inches — 74 cm — a day (NPS). Stevens Glacier was gone as of 2021, per NPS scientists. The glaciers keep retreating.

## Tahoma: more than 9,000 years of use

Six tribes hold ancestral ties to the mountain: the Cowlitz, Muckleshoot, Nisqually, Puyallup, Squaxin Island, and Yakama, along with Coast Salish peoples. Archaeologists have documented more than 9,000 years of human use and over 100 recorded sites around the mountain (NPS). These are not backdrop. The tribes named the peak long before it was Rainier — Tahoma, Takhoma, and other renderings. The Puyallup gloss is often given as "mother of waters," one interpretation; translations differ across languages and sources. The name itself is contested: Tacoma or Rainier, a dispute the record documents.

## 1870: Van Trump, Stevens, and Sluiskin

P.B. Van Trump and Hazard Stevens made the first recorded summit climb in 1870. A Native guide named Sluiskin led them to a basecamp near Paradise. Secondary sources often call Sluiskin Yakama; the NPS records only "a native guide." Fay Fuller, a Tacoma schoolteacher, became the first woman to reach the summit in 1890. Climbers made the first winter ascent in 1922. Ome Daiber, Arnold Campbell, and Will Borrow first climbed Liberty Ridge in 1935.

## March 2, 1899: the fifth national park

John Muir climbed to the summit in 1888. As Sierra Club president, he pressed for the mountain's preservation. The Pacific Forest Reserve was created in 1893, the year geologist Bailey Willis began campaigning for a park. Congress made Mount Rainier the nation's fifth national park, and President McKinley signed the legislation on March 2, 1899. From 1933 to 1941, the Civilian Conservation Corps built and repaired the park's trails, structures, and shelters. The corps worked the mountain for eight years. More than two million people visit each year (NPS).

## The summit caves and the lahar valleys

The twin summit craters hold the largest known glaciovolcanic cave system in the world (NPS; Journal of Cave and Karst Studies, 2023). Inside the East Crater ice, surveyors mapped a subglacial lake at about 14,049 feet. The lake sits under the ice, not in an open crater. The USGS calls Mount Rainier one of the most dangerous volcanoes in the United States. The IAVCEI lists it among the world's Decade Volcanoes. Lahars, not lava, are the greatest hazard — fast mudflows that pour down the river valleys. About 80,000 people and their homes lie in those hazard zones (USGS). They run downhill, toward the towns, away from the peak.

Back at Paradise, the glaciers feed the rivers that run to Puget Sound. The mountain that fills the sky is the same one that fills the valleys below.
