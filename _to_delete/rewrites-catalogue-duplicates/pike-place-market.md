## Facts corrected

1. **designating eight wagon spaces on Pike Place for farmers**
   - Problem: No authoritative source states the City designated 'eight wagon spaces.' The number 'eight' is the count of farmers who showed up opening day, not a designated allotment.
   - Corrected to: About eight farmers brought wagons/carts to Pike Place on opening day, August 17, 1907. Do not assert 'eight designated wagon spaces.'
   - Source: HistoryLink.org Essay 1949 (Greg Lange) *(editorial-secondary)*
2. **farmers sold out their entire inventory by noon ... overwhelming success**
   - Problem: Booster framing. The sourced account: only about eight farmers came, some sold out early, and many buyers left disappointed because there was too little produce.
   - Corrected to: So few farmers (about eight) attended that produce sold out early and many would-be buyers were turned away disappointed. Do not present as a clean success story.
   - Source: HistoryLink.org Essay 1949 (Greg Lange) *(editorial-secondary)*
3. **the original Starbucks (implying the 1971 store was at 1912 Pike Place)**
   - Problem: The 1971 store was NOT at 1912 Pike Place; it opened at 2000 Western Avenue and moved to 1912 Pike Place around 1976.
   - Corrected to: First Starbucks opened April 1971 at 2000 Western Avenue near the Market; the store at 1912 Pike Place (today marketed as 'the original') is the c.1976 relocation site.
   - Source: HistoryLink.org Essay 20292 *(editorial-secondary)*
4. **The Pike Place Market Preservation and Development Authority, created by the 1971 ballot i**
   - Problem: Wrong. The Nov. 2, 1971 initiative created the Historical District; the PDA was created separately by City charter in 1973.
   - Corrected to: The 1971 initiative created the seven-acre Historical District; the City chartered the PDA in 1973.
   - Source: PDA 50th Anniversary page; Seattle.gov *(primary-official)*
5. **Over 10 million annual visitors**
   - Problem: No authoritative source states a specific annual visitor count; 10-15 million figures circulate only in tourism/booster copy.
   - Corrected to: Drop the hard figure or attribute it as a tourism estimate. Prefer PDA's sourced descriptors (220+ shops, 70+ farmers, etc.).
   - Source: No authoritative source located *(editorial-secondary)*
6. **one of the oldest continuously operated public farmers' markets in the United States**
   - Problem: Superlative; PDA wording is 'one of the oldest and largest continuously operating public markets' - 'public markets,' not 'farmers' markets,' and a significance claim to attribute.
   - Corrected to: Attribute: 'The PDA describes Pike Place Market as one of the oldest and largest continuously operating public markets in the United States.'
   - Source: Pike Place Market PDA, 'About Pike Place Market' *(primary-official)*

## New material found in research

- Opened August 17, 1907, after Councilman Thomas P. Revelle's ordinance (passed August 5, 1907) let farmers sell directly to bypass commission-house middlemen (HistoryLink 1949).
- Opening day was rocky, not triumphant: only about eight farmers came; produce sold out fast and many buyers were turned away - a sourced, honest hook.
- 1960s urban-renewal threat (Pike Plaza Project) countered by Victor Steinbrueck (UW architecture professor) and 'Friends of the Market' (formed 1964).
- November 2, 1971 voter initiative (73,369-53,264) created the seven-acre Historical District; the City chartered the PDA in 1973 to run it.
- Pike Place Fish Market since 1930; John Yokoyama bought it in 1965 for $3,500; branded 'world famous' in 1986; fish-tossing documented at the Market as early as 1938 (HistoryLink 22703).
- First Starbucks opened April 1971 at 2000 Western Avenue and moved to 1912 Pike Place c.1976 (HistoryLink 20292).
- PDA-sourced scale descriptors that replace the fuzzy '10 million visitors': 220+ independently owned shops & restaurants, 180+ craftspeople, 70+ farmers, 60+ permitted buskers, 450+ affordable-housing residents.
