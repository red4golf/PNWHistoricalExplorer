## Facts corrected

1. **reducing the mountain's height from 12,000 feet to approximately 8,157 feet [caldera rim]**
   - Problem: Overstated/imprecise. The highest point on the caldera rim (NPS) is Hillman Peak at 8,151 ft; the highest point in the park overall is Mount Scott at 8,929 ft (not on the rim).
   - Corrected to: Highest point on the caldera rim: Hillman Peak, ~8,151 ft. (Highest point in the park: Mount Scott, 8,929 ft.)
   - Source: NPS Crater Lake nature page *(primary-official)*
2. **more than 40 times as powerful as the 1980 Mount St. Helens eruption**
   - Problem: USGS's own comparison is ~100x by new-magma volume (50 km3 vs ~0.5 km3). '40x' only holds against St. Helens' total bulk tephra; do not present it as the USGS figure.
   - Corrected to: ~50 km3 of magma, roughly 100 times the volume of new magma erupted by Mount St. Helens in 1980 (USGS).
   - Source: USGS Fact Sheet, Crater Lake: Presently Tranquil *(primary-official)*
3. **volcanic material reaching as far as central Canada**
   - Problem: Overreaching. Ashfall covered much of the Pacific Northwest and parts of southern/western Canada; the Mazama tephra is a marker bed across western Canada (Alberta, Saskatchewan), not 'central Canada.'
   - Corrected to: Ashfall blanketed much of the Pacific Northwest and parts of western Canada (the Mazama ash is a marker bed traced across Alberta and Saskatchewan).
   - Source: USGS Crater Lake pages; USGS Holocene tephra study *(primary-official)*
4. **the deepest freshwater lake in the United States**
   - Problem: Not NPS's phrasing; the 'freshwater' qualifier is unnecessary.
   - Corrected to: the deepest lake in the United States (and the deepest volcanic lake in the world).
   - Source: NPS Crater Lake FAQ *(primary-official)*
5. **obsidian tools and sagebrush bark sandals found buried beneath layers of volcanic ash, con**
   - Problem: Conflation/overstatement. The Fort Rock sandals were found at Fort Rock Cave (SE Oregon), not on Mazama's slopes; being under Mazama ash proves human presence in the region before the eruption, not that anyone 'witnessed' it. Witnessing is attested separately, by NPS, via Klamath/Umpqua oral tradition.
   - Corrected to: Artifacts such as the Fort Rock sagebrush-bark sandals, found buried beneath Mazama ash, confirm people lived in the region long before the eruption; NPS reports that Klamath and Umpqua oral traditions indicate people witnessed the eruption itself.
   - Source: OE, Fort Rock Sandals; NPS Traditional Knowledge *(editorial-secondary)*

## New material found in research

- The Llao vs. Skell battle over the chief's daughter (Loha), in NPS's own words including 'red hot rocks as large as hills' and the mountain collapsing on Llao - vivid and directly quotable from a primary-official source.
- The Fort Rock sandals: sagebrush-bark footwear ~9,100-10,400 years old, found stratified BENEATH Mazama ash - tangible, datable proof of deep-time human presence before the eruption.
- NPS's restrained framing that oral traditions 'indicate that people witnessed the eruption' - the responsible way to connect legend to geology.
- The dispossession arc: 1864 treaty and 23M-acre cession, 1902 park created without tribal consent, 1954 termination, 1986 restoration.
- The scale contrast: ~50 km3 of magma, ~100x the new magma of the 1980 St. Helens eruption, pyroclastic flows 70 km down-valley, ash forming a marker bed across the PNW into western Canada.
