## Facts corrected

1. **In August 1859, both nations agreed to a joint military occupation**
   - Problem: August 1859 was the PEAK of the armed standoff, not the moment of agreement. Baynes arrived Aug 5; by Aug 31 there were 461 U.S. troops facing the British squadron.
   - Corrected to: The standoff peaked in August 1859; Gen. Winfield Scott negotiated de-escalation in the fall of 1859; joint military occupation was approved November 1859. British Royal Marines formally established English Camp on March 21, 1860.
   - Source: NPS, 'The Pig War' and 'Harney Re-Examined Part III' *(primary-official)*
2. **Rear Admiral Robert Lambert Baynes arrived with five warships carrying 2,140 men and 70 gu**
   - Problem: The precise '2,140 men and 70 guns' figures are commonly-cited secondary numbers, not confirmed verbatim in the authoritative NPS text (which supports roughly five warships and nearly 2,000 Royal Marines).
   - Corrected to: Use the NPS-supported scale: roughly five British warships and nearly 2,000 Royal Marines against 461 U.S. soldiers. Attribute the precise 2,140/70 figures to Vouri if used, or drop them.
   - Source: NPS, 'The Pig War' *(primary-official)*
3. **he reportedly stated he would not embroil two great nations in a war over a squabble about**
   - Problem: NPS attributes the sentiment to Baynes but the polished wording is a traditional paraphrase; his documented reaction was blunter ('tut, tut, no, no, the damn fools').
   - Corrected to: Present the 'two great nations ... over a squabble about a pig' line as attributed/traditional, not a verified verbatim transcript.
   - Source: NPS, 'Lambert Baynes' *(primary-official)*

## New material found in research

- The recurring-trespass detail: an HBC Hawaiian (Kanaka) herdsman named Jacob saw the pig in Cutlar's garden - not its first raid (NPS).
- The economics of the quarrel: Cutlar offered about $10; Griffin demanded $100 (about 6x the pig's value) (NPS).
- The mismatch at the peak: 461 U.S. soldiers with a handful of cannon against roughly five British warships and nearly 2,000 Royal Marines, held in check by one admiral's restraint (NPS).
- Baynes's restraint: NPS records both the famous 'two great nations ... squabble about a pig' line and his blunter documented reaction, 'tut, tut, no, no, the damn fools.'
- The arbitration twist: Kaiser Wilhelm I of Germany settled an Anglo-American border in the Pacific Northwest; ruling October 21, 1872 for the U.S. via Haro Strait.
- The contrast between the surviving camps: English Camp's sheltered formal garden, blockhouse and cemetery vs. American Camp's windswept prairie above the Strait of Juan de Fuca (NPS).
- Coast Salish reef-net fishing as the deep-time backdrop reframes the 'Pig War' as one brief chapter on long-inhabited islands (NPS).
