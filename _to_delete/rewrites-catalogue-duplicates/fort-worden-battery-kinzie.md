## Facts corrected

1. **protected Puget Sound from 1897 to 1953 / commanded the north arm of the Triangle of Fire **
   - Problem: 1897 is when construction began, not when the fort was operational. The fort was activated May 14, 1902.
   - Corrected to: Active/garrisoned defense period was 1902-1953 (construction began 1897).
   - Source: Washington State Parks, 'Fort Worden Historical State Park History' *(primary-official)*
2. **The guns fell silent in 1944 when changing military technology made fixed coastal defenses**
   - Problem: The 1944 date is unsupported; the fort's guns were decommissioned in 1945 and removed in 1946 (an earlier batch left in 1918 for WWI).
   - Corrected to: Last guns decommissioned 1945, removed 1946; fort deactivated 1953.
   - Source: Washington State Parks Foundation, Fort Worden Artillery Hill tour *(primary-official)*
3. **This sophisticated optical system, manufactured in Paris by Barbier, Benard & Turenne**
   - Problem: No Point Wilson-specific source names this maker; this is the known copy-paste error. Reliable sources describe only a fourth-order Fresnel lens without identifying a manufacturer.
   - Corrected to: Fourth-order Fresnel lens (c.1894, formerly at Point Bonita); manufacturer not confirmed - drop the maker attribution.
   - Source: HistoryLink.org File 5680; Lighthousefriends (finding-aid only) *(editorial-secondary)*
4. **a fourth-order Fresnel lens that created a powerful beam visible for 17 nautical miles**
   - Problem: Range overstated; HistoryLink gives 16 miles.
   - Corrected to: Visible for about 16 miles.
   - Source: HistoryLink.org File 5680 *(editorial-secondary)*
5. **Batteries Ash, Powell, Brannon, Quarles, Randol, Vicars, Tolles, Stoddard, Benson, Putnam,**
   - Problem: Battery name misspelled.
   - Corrected to: Battery Brannan (not 'Brannon').
   - Source: Coast Defense Study Group; Washington State Parks Foundation *(editorial-secondary)*
6. **the current concrete tower was built in 1914 as part of the comprehensive coastal defense **
   - Problem: The light station (est. 1879) predates the fort (activated 1902); the 1914 tower was a Lighthouse Service replacement, not a military coastal-defense structure.
   - Corrected to: The 1914 tower was a Lighthouse Service replacement of the 1879 station, located within (predating) Fort Worden.
   - Source: HistoryLink.org File 5680 *(editorial-secondary)*

## New material found in research

- Battery Kinzie: two 12-inch M1895 guns on disappearing carriages, named for Brig. Gen. David H. Kinzie (d. July 5, 1904) - the fort's heaviest emplacement (CDSG).
- Agency-sourced chronology: construction began July 1897, activated May 14, 1902, became HQ of the Harbor Defenses of Puget Sound Sept 4, 1904, deactivated June 30, 1953 (Washington State Parks).
- Triangle of Fire - Fort Worden, Fort Casey, Fort Flagler creating overlapping fields of fire at Admiralty Inlet (HistoryLink File 7524).
- Guns never fired in anger: about half pulled in 1918 for WWI, last decommissioned 1945 and removed 1946 (WA State Parks Foundation).
- Point Wilson Lighthouse: light since Dec 15, 1879; current 46-foot octagonal concrete tower built 1914; fourth-order Fresnel lens; automated Nov 1976 (HistoryLink File 5680; LOC HABS/HAER wa0724).
- Film history: 'An Officer and a Gentleman' filmed at Fort Worden April-June 1981, released 1982 (HistoryLink File 8237).
- Second life: dedicated as a state park Aug 18, 1973; now a conference center and home of the Puget Sound Coast Artillery Museum (WA State Parks).
