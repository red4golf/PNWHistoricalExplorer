## New material found in research

- NPS: Modoc War casualties by category — 53 U.S. soldiers, 17 civilians, 2 Warm Springs Scouts, 5 Modoc women/children, 15 Modoc warriors (5 killed in battle) (home.nps.gov/labe/learn/historyculture/modoc-war.htm)
- NPS: Battle of the Stronghold, January 17, 1873 — over 300 soldiers attacked Captain Jack's Stronghold and were repelled
- NPS: April 26, 1873 — Scarfaced Charley's Modoc fighters defeated a 69-soldier patrol, killing both commanding officers (the Thomas-Wright Massacre)
- NPS: April 11, 1873 — Modoc warriors killed Gen. Edward R.S. Canby and Rev. Eleazar Thomas during the peace parley
- NPS: Captain Jack (Kintpuash) hanged October 3, 1873 at Fort Klamath, with Schonchin John, Black Jim, and Boston Charley
- NPS: approximately 150 Modoc men, women and children sheltered in Captain Jack's Stronghold for about five months including winter
- NPS: 163 Modocs exiled to the Quapaw Agency in Indian Territory (Oklahoma); population declined by about one-third, largely from tuberculosis
- NPS Foundation Document: monument proclaimed by President Calvin Coolidge on November 21, 1925; 46,692 acres, 94% in Siskiyou County; NPS assumed management 1933; 28,460 acres (61%) designated wilderness in 1972
- NPS Foundation Document: more than 750 documented lava tube caves in the monument's basalt and andesite flows (nps.gov main page separately says 'more than 800 caves' — both are official NPS figures, count varies by source/date)
- NPS: Petroglyph Point holds over 5,000 individual carvings in volcanic tuff; some images estimated over 6,000 years old; site is the center of a Modoc creation story
