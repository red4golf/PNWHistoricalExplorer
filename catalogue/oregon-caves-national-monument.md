## Facts corrected (found during research)

1. **Oregon Caves began forming over 190 million years ago when the area lay beneath an ancient**
   - Problem: Conflates the age of the parent rock with the age of the cave itself. The limestone that became the cave's marble did originate as ancient seafloor deposits, but at roughly 210 million years ago (Late Triassic), and it was metamorphosed to marble around 170–160 million years ago — not 190 million. Critically, the cave passages themselves are far younger: dissolution did not begin until marble near the surface fractured, roughly 1.7 million years ago.
   - Corrected to: The limestone (later marble) originated as seamount deposits about 210 million years ago (Late Triassic); it was metamorphosed via accretion of the Rattlesnake Creek terrane (~170 million years ago) and intrusion of the Grayback pluton (~160 million years ago). The cave itself — the actual dissolved passages — began forming about 1.7 million years ago.
   - Source: NPS Geodiversity Atlas, nps.gov/articles/nps-geodiversity-atlas-oregon-caves-national-monument-preserve-oregon.htm; Oregon Secretary of State Blue Book, 'Oregon Caves National Monument and Preserve' by state geologist Jason McClaughry, sos.oregon.gov/blue-book *(primary-official)*
2. **Walter Burch began offering guided cave tours in the 1880s, establishing the foundation fo**
   - Problem: No graded source supports the specific claims of Burch installing 'lighting systems and safety equipment' or formally 'training guides.' The documented history is narrower and more concrete than this generic summary.
   - Corrected to: Walter Burch first heard about the cave in 1884 and formed an exploring party; with brothers-in-law Homer and Ernest Harkness he spent $1,500 and three years trying to secure land rights, ultimately obtaining only squatter's rights. He opened the cave to the public in 1885 under the name 'the Limestone Caves.' During exploration he reportedly spent two days in ice water blasting open a passage into what is now called the Niagara Falls chamber, and was the first person to see the Grand Column.
   - Source: NPS, 'Walter Burch,' nps.gov/orca/learn/historyculture/walterburch.htm; NPS Oregon Caves timeline *(primary-official)*
3. **The Oregon Caves Chateau, built in 1934 by contractor Gust Lium...**
   - Problem: Understates the construction story and Lium's role; he was a self-taught local builder/architect (not simply 'contractor'), and construction spanned a specific multi-year period with documented cost and crew size worth citing.
   - Corrected to: Construction began in September 1931 and was completed in May 1934 (about 2.5 years, slowed by Depression-era financial setbacks in 1932–33). Gust Lium, a self-taught Grants Pass builder/architect who had completed over 50 houses in the region, led a crew of fewer than 20 men; the project cost about $50,000. Materials included Port Orford cedar, local fir, pine, madrone and oak, and stone gathered from nearby hillsides; Caves Creek was channeled through the six-story building's dining room. The Chateau was designated a National Historic Landmark in October 1987.
   - Source: NPS, 'Gust Lium'; NPS Oregon Caves timeline; Oregon Encyclopedia, 'Oregon Caves Chateau' (attribute in text) — oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/oregon_caves_chateau/ *(primary-official)*
4. **Native peoples likely viewed the caves with spiritual significance, as underground spaces **
   - Problem: Speculative claim with no citable source; per the accuracy rule this should be dropped rather than presented as likely fact. NPS states plainly there is no evidence Native Americans even entered the cave, let alone ascribed spiritual meaning to its interior.
   - Corrected to: DROP this sentence. What can be stated: the Takelma inhabited the Illinois and Rogue River valleys (including the general area) for thousands of years, but 'there is at this time no concrete evidence that they entered or used the cave' (NPS).
   - Source: NPS, 'Takelma,' nps.gov/orca/learn/historyculture/takelma.htm *(primary-official)*

## New material found in research

- Elijah Davidson's 1874 discovery: his dog Bruno chased an animal into a hidden opening; Davidson entered with only Chinese sulfur matches for light, spent more than three hours lost in the dark, and navigated back out by following the sound of the stream, on hands and knees (NPS, 'Elijah Davidson's Story' by Tom Siewert).
- Walter Burch reportedly spent two days working in ice water to blast open a passage into what is now called the Niagara Falls chamber, and was the first person to see the Grand Column (NPS, 'Walter Burch').
- Joaquin Miller visited in 1907, coined 'Marble Halls of Oregon,' and lobbied for federal protection — the monument was proclaimed two years later, in 1909 (Oregon History Project, attributed).
- President William Howard Taft proclaimed the 480-acre Oregon Caves National Monument on July 12, 1909, under the Antiquities Act (NPS timeline).
- The Oregon Caves Chateau: built by self-taught local builder Gust Lium, September 1931–May 1934, cost about $50,000, crew of fewer than 20 men, six stories with 23 rooms, with Caves Creek channeled through the dining room; designated a National Historic Landmark in October 1987 (NPS).
- The cave is a single, continuous system of about 4.8 km (3 miles) of interconnected passages — the longest marble solution cave in the Siskiyou Mountains (NPS Geodiversity Atlas).
- The oldest dated cave formation, a flowstone in Neptune's Grotto, is about 370,000 years old; jaguar bones found in the cave in 1995 were dated to about 38,600 years old, and grizzly bear bones found the same year to over 50,000 years old (NPS timeline; Oregon Blue Book).
- In 2014, Congress added a 4,070-acre national preserve to the monument, signed into law by President Obama on December 19, 2014, bringing the total protected area to roughly 4,554 acres — a fact entirely absent from the current entry.
- Bats using the cave include Townsend's big-eared bat, the Yuma bat, and the long-eared myotis; a small number hibernate there in winter, and bat gates have been installed partly to guard against white-nose syndrome (NPS, 'Bats').
- 1968: Oregon Caves recorded its millionth visitor and its first female tour guide began work the same year (NPS timeline).
