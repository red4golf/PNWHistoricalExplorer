## Facts corrected

1. **~51,000 workers total over the war; peak simultaneous workforce ~45,000.**
   - Problem: Conflates two figures. ~51,000 is the mid-1944 employee count (the number who donated a day's pay), not a war-long total.
   - Corrected to: Peak simultaneous workforce roughly 45,000-50,000; roughly 100,000 recruited over 1943-1945 due to high turnover. Do not call 51,000 the war-long total.
   - Source: NPS, 'About Hanford'; Atomic Heritage *(primary-official)*
2. **~4,000 pounds of uranium to produce one pound of plutonium.**
   - Problem: Garbled ratio.
   - Corrected to: Per NPS: about 3,000 kg of uranium yielded roughly 750 grams of plutonium (about 0.025 percent). Prefer the NPS kg->grams statement over a pounds ratio.
   - Source: NPS, 'Science of the B Reactor at Hanford' *(primary-official)*
3. **the B-17 was accepted by Brig. Gen. James E. Parker.**
   - Problem: Not verifiable and conflicts with the best account.
   - Corrected to: Per HistoryLink, the plane was christened by Katie Belle Harris (a Gold Star mother) and accepted by Colonel David Wade, base commander at Walla Walla Army Air Field. Remove the Parker attribution.
   - Source: HistoryLink.org, File 7491 *(editorial-secondary)*
4. **flew ~67 combat missions.**
   - Problem: The precise number 67 is unsupported.
   - Corrected to: State 'more than 60 combat missions.' Drop the precise 67 unless a primary squadron record is found.
   - Source: HistoryLink.org, File 7491 *(editorial-secondary)*
5. **returned to US July 10, 1945.**
   - Problem: Sourced account gives only the month.
   - Corrected to: Returned to the U.S. in July 1945 (day not confirmed).
   - Source: HistoryLink.org, File 7491 *(editorial-secondary)*
6. **56 million gallons ... ~one-third leaking.**
   - Problem: Tense/precision error; roughly a third have leaked over time, only a few are actively leaking now.
   - Corrected to: Of the 177 tanks, at least 67 (about a third) are assumed to have leaked in the past, and a few are currently leaking. Phrase as 'about a third have leaked over time.'
   - Source: Washington State Department of Ecology, 'Hanford overview' *(primary-official)*
7. **cleanup ... tank waste by 2040, full remediation ~2091, >$100 billion.**
   - Problem: Outdated and imprecise.
   - Corrected to: Per the 2025 Hanford Lifecycle Scope, Schedule and Cost Report: baseline remaining cleanup cost about $364 billion, up to about $589 billion with uncertainty; active cleanup projected through roughly FY2086 with long-term stewardship to about FY2100; tank-waste treatment mission about FY2079. The 2040 date refers only to retrieving waste from 22 single-shell tanks in the 200 West Area, not all tank waste.
   - Source: Hanford Lifecycle Scope, Schedule and Cost Report, 2025 (hanford.gov) *(primary-official)*
8. **Hanford Site ~586 square miles.**
   - Problem: Sources round differently.
   - Corrected to: Roughly 580-586 square miles (DOE 580; NPS about 600; EPA/DOE commonly 586).
   - Source: DOE 'Hanford Site by Numbers'; NPS 'About Hanford' *(primary-official)*

## New material found in research

- B Reactor: world's first full-scale plutonium production reactor; graphite-moderated, water-cooled; 2,004 aluminum process tubes; about 75,000 gal/min cooling water; critical September 26, 1944 (DOE + NPS).
- Site chosen early 1943 for hydroelectric power, Columbia River water, and isolation; roughly 580-586 sq mi.
- Displacement: about 1,500 residents given about 30 days after a March 6, 1943 notice; towns razed; Wanapum lost river access; treaty tribes Yakama, Umatilla, Nez Perce affected.
- Nine reactors 1943-1987; plutonium for Trinity (July 16, 1945) and Fat Man/Nagasaki (Aug 9, 1945).
- Environmental legacy: 56 million gallons in 177 tanks, at least 67 having leaked, a groundwater plume; remaining cleanup now about $364-589 billion running to roughly FY2086-2100.
- Preservation: B Reactor a National Historic Landmark; Manhattan Project NHP established Nov. 10, 2015.
- Day's Pay B-17G human-interest anchor: Max Blanchard's idea, 51,000 workers' one-day wages (about $300,000), christened at Hanford Field July 23, 1944, scrapped Dec. 4, 1947 - with corrected accepting officer (Col. David Wade) and 'more than 60' missions.
