## New material found in research

- The 1855 Capitol fire happened on the night of December 31, 1855; the Oregon Blue Book (Oregon Secretary of State, primary/official) states its destruction "was considered an incendiary part of" the political controversy over whether the capital would be Salem or Corvallis, and the Oregon Encyclopedia (editorial-secondary) independently describes the building as having "burned under suspicious circumstances in 1855."
- The 1935 fire that destroyed the second Capitol broke out on the night of April 25, 1935; per the Oregon Encyclopedia, it was "generally believed the fire was accidental and that it originated in the storeroom-service area in the basement" — a specific, sourceable origin point the current entry omits.
- The 1876 Capitol's architects were Justus F. Krumbein and W.G. Gilbert (firm Krumbein & Gilbert), with later phases overseen by W.W. Piper, Wilbur F. Boothby, and Delos D. Neer, and Krumbein returning in 1892 to modify the dome — named individuals the current draft leaves generic (Oregon Encyclopedia, editorial-secondary).
- The 1876 building's dome reached roughly 190 feet, was copper-clad, and was explicitly modeled on Thomas U. Walter's cast-iron dome added to the U.S. Capitol between 1859 and 1863 (Oregon Encyclopedia, editorial-secondary) — a concrete architectural lineage fact absent from the draft.
- The 1938 Capitol design competition drew 123 entries nationwide (more than twenty from Oregon firms) and was judged double-blind by a citizens' committee (Oregon Historical Quarterly, Willingham, peer-reviewed, and corroborated by SAH Archipedia).
- 158 named individuals are inscribed in the rotunda friezes above the House and Senate chambers; the list was compiled in 1938 as the building neared completion and draws on Philip Cogswell Jr.'s 1977 book 'Capitol Names: Individuals Woven Into Oregon's History' (Oregon State Capitol Foundation, primary/official).
- The Oregon Pioneer statue is gilded bronze, 23 feet tall, sculpted by Ulric Ellerhusen, atop the building's lantern (confirmed independently by Oregon Historical Quarterly/peer-reviewed, Oregon State Capitol Foundation/primary, and the Oregon Legislature's oregoncapitol.com/primary) — the frontmatter's '23-karat gold-leafed' description is not supported by any graded source and should be dropped or changed to 'gilded bronze.'
- Total building height to the lantern pinnacle is given as 168 feet by both the Oregon State Capitol Foundation (primary/official) and the Oregon Encyclopedia (editorial-secondary), versus the draft's 166-foot figure — needs reconciling before publication.
- The Willson Park portion of the Capitol grounds was originally platted in 1846 by William Willson, a Methodist lay member, with the land donated by William and Chloe Willson in 1853; the combined Capitol Mall covers 21 acres (Oregon State Parks brochure, primary/official).
- The Circuit Rider statue in the Capitol grounds was dedicated in 1924 by a son in honor of his father, Robert Booth, and 'honors preachers who rode circuits carrying the gospel to remote communities' (Oregon State Parks brochure, primary/official) — more specific and verifiable than the draft's generic claim about 'Methodist ministers.'
