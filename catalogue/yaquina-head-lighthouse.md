## New material found in research

- Named date/named person: President Andrew Johnson signed the Executive Order on June 8, 1866, that withdrew 19 acres of the headland for lighthouse purposes (BLM primary).
- Named date: Captain James Cook sighted and logged the headland on March 7, 1778 (BLM primary).
- Correction: the tower's 370,000-plus bricks were NOT made on site — they were manufactured by the Patent Brick Company of San Rafael, California, and shipped in (Oregon Encyclopedia, attributed).
- The Cape Foulweather story is a documented misconception, not a materials mix-up: OE's Cameron La Follette writes that early General Land Office records and survey plats sometimes mislabeled the headland 'Cape Foulweather,' but research confirms the Lighthouse Board always targeted Yaquina Head — no evidence materials were sent to the wrong site.
- Two boats were lost attempting to deliver building materials to the site during construction (OE, attributed) — concrete, sourced hardship detail.
- Tower ironwork was manufactured in Philadelphia and completed in June 1872 by Oregon Iron Works of Clackamas, Oregon (OE, attributed).
- First-order Fresnel lens built in Paris in 1868 by Barbier & Fenestre; arrived on site October 1872, but start-up was delayed when pieces of the lantern's shipping crate were lost (OE, attributed).
- During WWII the Coast Guard stationed seventeen servicemen at Yaquina Head to watch for enemy ships and submarines (OE, attributed).
- Common murre colony: OSU's Seabird Oceanography Lab reports more than 50,000 common murres may breed on the cliffs and islands in some years, alongside pigeon guillemots, Western gulls, pelagic and Brandt's cormorants, and black oystercatchers; OSU has monitored the colony since 1998 (peer-reviewed source).
- Gray whales pass Yaquina Head on southbound migration to Baja California (late Dec-Jan) and northbound migration to the Bering Sea (March-April), with some resident whales year-round; harbor seals live there year-round in Quarry Cove/around Seal Island (BLM brochure, primary).
