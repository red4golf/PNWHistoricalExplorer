## Facts corrected

1. **10 generators producing 526.7 megawatts of electricity**
   - Problem: 526.7 MW is the ORIGINAL first-powerhouse rating (Oregon Encyclopedia), not the operator's current figure. USACE currently lists the first powerhouse at 518 MW nameplate (574 MW overload).
   - Corrected to: First powerhouse 518 MW nameplate (574 MW overload) per USACE; if citing the original 1938-44 rating, attribute it ('about 526.7 MW as originally rated').
   - Source: USACE Northwestern Division Fact Sheet; The Oregon Encyclopedia *(primary-official)*
2. **stretching 2,690 feet across the Columbia River**
   - Problem: 2,690 ft is sourced only to Wikipedia; USACE gives the dam length as 2,477 feet. The structure spans two channels separated by Bradford and Cascades Islands, so a single 'length across the Columbia' is measured inconsistently.
   - Corrected to: About 2,477 feet (USACE); or describe the multi-channel structure rather than citing 2,690 ft as authoritative.
   - Source: USACE Northwestern Division Fact Sheet; NPS/NRHP Nomination *(primary-official)*

## New material found in research

- Woody Guthrie's May 1941 BPA commission - roughly 26 songs in 30 days, including 'Roll On, Columbia' - a vivid, well-documented human story.
- New Deal make-work origins: ~3,000 peak workers, over a million cubic yards of concrete, the dam as a Depression employment engine, placed in service June 6, 1938.
- The drowned Cascades Rapids: a specific, place-based account of Indigenous dispossession that grounds the abstract 'impact on Native fisheries' - and is distinct from Celilo.
- The public-power fight: BPA's 1937 creation and its 'preference' clause for public bodies and cooperatives; cheap hydropower fueling the WWII aluminum industry.
- The two-powerhouse evolution: 1938 first powerhouse (10 generators), 1981 second powerhouse, 1993 new lock; today 18 units, over 1,200 MW; NHL 1987.
