## Facts corrected (found during research)

1. **Beginning in the 1940s, Dutch immigrants transformed the fertile Skagit Valley into one of**
   - Problem: This erases the actual origin of the industry. Per HistoryLink.org, commercial bulb growing in the valley began in 1906 with Mary Brown Stewart (an American grower), and it was not until World War II era that Dutch immigrant bulb growers arrived and the industry 'proliferated.' The 1940s date applies to the surge of Dutch arrivals and industry expansion, not the industry's founding.
   - Corrected to: Commercial tulip/bulb growing in the Skagit Valley began in 1906 (Mary Brown Stewart); the industry expanded significantly after World War II with the arrival of Dutch bulb growers.
   - Source: HistoryLink.org, 'Farming in the Skagit Valley,' File #21283 *(editorial-secondary)*
2. **The Skagit Valley Tulip Festival, now in its 42nd year...**
   - Problem: The festival launched in 1984. 2025 was widely reported as the 42nd annual festival, meaning by 2026 (the current year) it would be the 43rd — this phrasing goes stale immediately and was already outdated by the time of writing. Better to state the founding year and let readers compute, or explicitly say '43rd' for 2026.
   - Corrected to: The Skagit Valley Tulip Festival, launched in 1984 (in 2026, its 43rd year)...
   - Source: HistoryLink.org (1984 founding); AllEvents.in listing corroborating 2025 as the 42nd annual festival *(editorial-secondary)*
3. **Their company, RoozenGaarde, became the largest bulb grower in North America, eventually c**
   - Problem: The '200 varieties on 50+ acres' figure is confirmed by RoozenGaarde's own site, but 'largest bulb grower in North America' is not the superlative any graded source actually makes. A 2025 goskagit.com article instead describes the Roozen family as operating 'the largest family business in the world that grows tulips, daffodils and irises' — a different and narrower claim that itself needs in-text attribution as a superlative.
   - Corrected to: RoozenGaarde/Washington Bulb Company cultivates approximately 200 tulip varieties and 50 daffodil varieties across 50+ acres of fields plus a 5+ acre display garden (RoozenGaarde's own figures); goskagit.com (2025) describes the Roozen family as running 'the largest family business in the world that grows tulips, daffodils and irises' — attribute this claim if used.
   - Source: tulips.com (primary, RoozenGaarde's own site); goskagit.com, 'The Washington Bulb Company in the business of producing tulips' (editorial-secondary, for the superlative) *(primary-official)*
4. **Tulip Town, founded in 1984, features an iconic windmill...**
   - Problem: No graded source confirms an exact 1984 founding date for Tulip Town specifically, or the windmill detail. The Spokesman-Review obituary for co-founder Tom DeGoede describes Tulip Town's origin as small tours/bouquet sales starting in 'the early 1980s,' not a specific 1984 founding, and does not mention a windmill.
   - Corrected to: Tulip Town grew out of small tours and bouquet sales that Tom and Jeannette DeGoede began offering at their farm in the early 1980s.
   - Source: The Spokesman-Review, June 5, 2019 *(editorial-secondary)*

## New material found in research

- 1906: Mary Brown Stewart planted tulips for commercial sale in Skagit County — the actual start of commercial bulb growing there, predating Dutch immigrant arrival by decades (HistoryLink.org)
- 1945: Hank DeGoede and Bill Roozen arrived from Holland, sponsored by Neil Noorlag (festival's own history page — attribute, not independently confirmed)
- 1955: Bill Roozen established the Washington Bulb Company (festival's own history page — attribute)
- Tom DeGoede emigrated from the Netherlands to Canada in 1956, later relocating to Skagit County to grow tulip bulbs with his brothers; died in 2019 at age 85 (The Spokesman-Review obituary, June 5, 2019)
- Tom and Jeannette DeGoede began offering small tours and selling bouquets at their farm in 'the early 1980s' — the origin of Tulip Town (The Spokesman-Review, June 5, 2019)
- The Skagit Valley Tulip Festival was introduced in 1984 (HistoryLink.org)
- By 2019 the festival drew an estimated 300,000 visitors and generated $65 million in annual revenue for the county (HistoryLink.org)
- By 2024 the festival drew about 600,000 visitors and generated an estimated $83 million (Cascadia Daily News, March 29, 2025)
- Today about 1,000 acres of tulips and daffodils are grown in Skagit County each year, yielding an estimated 20 million bulbs and 75 million cut flowers; Skagit County accounts for about 75% of U.S. commercial tulip production (HistoryLink.org)
- RoozenGaarde's own site describes its display garden as '5+ acre' plus 'over 50 acres of fields,' with 'approximately 200 tulip types and 50 daffodil varieties' (tulips.com, official site)
