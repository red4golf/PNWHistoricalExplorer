## New material found in research

- Steamer service between Eagle Harbor/Winslow and Seattle began in 1887 with the steamer Tolo, operated by Charles Williams (HistoryLink, editorially-reviewed).
- The steamer Eagle joined the run in 1901 and burned at the dock in 1903, ending its brief service — HistoryLink notes local lore attributed the loss to the ship having been christened with flowers instead of champagne (label as legend).
- In 1937 the car ferry route from Seattle moved from Port Blakely to Winslow, the same year passenger-steamer service to the island ended (HistoryLink).
- The Agate Pass Bridge, connecting Bainbridge Island to the Kitsap Peninsula, opened in October 1950 — the same year a new car-ferry dock was built at Winslow on the east end of town (HistoryLink).
- Washington State Ferries began statewide operations at 3:00 a.m. on June 1, 1951, taking over from the Puget Sound Navigation Company/Black Ball Line; the ferry Vashon became the first state-run sailing, from Lofall at 5:20 a.m. Governor Arthur Langlie and Highway Director William Bugge attended the changeover ceremonies (HistoryLink).
- Winslow incorporated as a fourth-class city on September 18, 1947, following an August 23, 1947 election that passed 122 to 110; population at incorporation was 905; Herbert Allen was the first mayor (HistoryLink).
- Voters approved annexing the entire island into the city of Winslow in November 1990, 3,193 to 3,057, on 71.5 percent turnout; the following year, November 7, 1991, residents voted to rename the city Bainbridge Island (HistoryLink).
- The current MV Tacoma (built 1997) and MV Wenatchee (built 1998, rebuilt 2025) are Jumbo Mark II-class ferries, each 460 feet 2 inches long, with a maximum capacity of 202 vehicles and 1,791 passengers (WSDOT official vessel data) — corrects the entry's claimed 2,500-passenger capacity.
- The Bainbridge terminal's overhead passenger walkway — a wooden structure over 50 years old with earthquake-vulnerable wood piles — was replaced by a wider concrete-and-steel, seismically code-built structure; four prefabricated spans were barged in and installed over six days in September, the new walkway opened February 5, 2024, and the combined walkway-replacement/cab-rehab project totaled $33 million (WSDOT official project page).
- James Ryan's family moved into a vacant cabin just east of today's Washington State Ferries dock in 1877; Riley Hoskinson, who arrived in 1878, served as a U.S. government 'voluntary observer' recording some of the first Washington-territory weather data, reporting to Washington, D.C. from 1878 to 1889 (HistoryLink).
