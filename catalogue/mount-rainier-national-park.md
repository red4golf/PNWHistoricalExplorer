## Facts corrected

1. **the world's largest volcanic glacier cave network within its ice-filled craters and the hi**
   - Problem: No authoritative source supports a 14,203-ft open crater lake or a 'highest crater lake in North America' superlative. The documented feature is a SUBGLACIAL lake ('Lake Adelie') inside the East Crater ice-cave, at ~14,049 ft per the peer-reviewed survey.
   - Corrected to: Recast as a subglacial lake within the summit ice-cave system (~14,049 ft); drop the '14,203 ft / highest crater lake in North America' claim.
   - Source: Journal of Cave and Karst Studies v.85 (2023) *(peer-reviewed)*
2. **one of the world's most dangerous volcanoes**
   - Problem: USGS's own wording is 'one of our Nation's most dangerous'; the 'world's' framing is defensible only via the IAVCEI Decade Volcano designation.
   - Corrected to: 'one of the most dangerous volcanoes in the United States (USGS)' or note its Decade Volcano designation.
   - Source: USGS FAQ; IAVCEI Decade Volcano list *(primary-official)*
3. **26 major glaciers**
   - Problem: Count is not stable across sources and is decreasing (25/26/27/28 across NPS and USGS; Stevens Glacier gone 2021).
   - Corrected to: 'about two dozen glaciers (roughly 25, with counts varying and declining).'
   - Source: NPS Fast Facts (25); USGS FAQ (~26); NPS glaciers page (28 named) *(primary-official)*
4. **measured moving 29 inches per day in May 1970**
   - Problem: The 29 in/day record is correct but authoritative sources do not tie it to 'May 1970.'
   - Corrected to: 'about 29 inches (74 cm) per day' - omit the unconfirmed 'May 1970' date.
   - Source: NPS Geologic Resource Evaluation Report *(primary-official)*

## New material found in research

- The headline superlatives, each attributable: 14,410 ft, greatest glacier ice cover in the lower 48, the world's largest volcanic glacier cave system in the summit craters (NPS + peer-reviewed).
- The hazard that matters: USGS calls it one of the nation's most dangerous volcanoes, with lahars threatening ~80,000 people in the valleys below.
- The 1870 first ascent by Van Trump and Stevens, guided by Sluiskin to a Paradise basecamp; Fay Fuller the first woman up, in 1890.
- The name: the tribes' Tahoma/Takhoma, one gloss 'mother of waters' (Puyallup), for a peak whose 26-odd glaciers feed the Puget Sound rivers - attributed, with translations noted to differ.
- The park-creation campaign: Muir's 1888 climb, Bailey Willis's advocacy, fifth national park signed March 2, 1899.
- Glaciers in retreat: Nisqually's record ~29 in/day movement; Stevens Glacier gone as of 2021.
