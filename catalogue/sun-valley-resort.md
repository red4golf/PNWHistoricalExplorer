## Facts corrected

1. **world's first destination ski resort**
   - Problem: Overstated. European destination winter/ski resorts long predate Sun Valley. Official and scholarly sources call it America's/North America's first.
   - Corrected to: America's (North America's) first destination ski resort.
   - Source: Sun Valley Resort official history; SAH Archipedia *(primary-official)*
2. **Bald Mountain developed for expert terrain post-war**
   - Problem: Baldy opened for alpine skiing in December 1939 - before, not after, WWII.
   - Corrected to: Bald Mountain opened for alpine skiing in December 1939 (pre-war), with continued expansion after the resort reopened in 1946.
   - Source: Sun Valley Resort official history; SAH Archipedia *(primary-official)*
3. **chairlift adapted from Union Pacific engineering used for loading bananas onto ships**
   - Problem: Should credit engineer James Curran and UP's Omaha shops rather than implying UP originated the banana-loading machinery.
   - Corrected to: The world's first chairlift, designed by UP engineer James Curran by adapting a conveyor system he had used to load bananas/produce onto ships, engineered at Union Pacific's Omaha shops.
   - Source: Union Pacific Railroad press release; University of Houston 'Engines of Our Ingenuity' No. 2953 *(primary-official)*

## New material found in research

- 1936 founding by Averell Harriman / Union Pacific to fill Depression-era passenger trains (UP Museum + official history).
- Count Felix Schaffgotsch's cross-West scouting mission ending in the Wood River Valley near Ketchum (SAH Archipedia).
- Birthplace of the chairlift: James Curran's banana-conveyor design, tested at UP's Omaha shops, opening on Dollar and Proctor mountains December 1936 (UP press release + Univ. of Houston).
- Sun Valley Lodge opened December 21, 1936, designed by national-park lodge architect Gilbert Stanley Underwood (SAH Archipedia).
- Hemingway thread: worked on 'For Whom the Bell Tolls' at the Lodge (Suite 206) in 1939; lived and died in Ketchum, July 2, 1961 (JFK Library).
- WWII service as a U.S. Navy convalescent hospital, 1942/43-1945 (SAH Archipedia + UP Museum).
- Correct geography: Wood River Valley, central Idaho; Baldy in the Smoky Mountains, adjacent to Ketchum.
