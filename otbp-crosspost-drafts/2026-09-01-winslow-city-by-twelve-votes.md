Step off the ferry on Bainbridge Island and you land at the east end of Winslow Way, in a downtown that still goes by Winslow. That is not a leftover from some old map nobody bothered to update. Winslow was a real city, with its own mayor and council, for forty-four years. Then, in 1991, the people who lived there voted to give that city a different name. Mostly, the old one went away. Not entirely.

**A Town Named for Trees**

Before it was Winslow, this stretch of Eagle Harbor was called Madrone, after the trees along the shore. A Kansas transplant named Riley Hoskinson settled here in 1878 and more or less got the place started — he ran the area's only weather station for twelve years, before Seattle even had one of its own. A one-room schoolhouse went up in 1881. The Eagle Harbor Congregational Church followed in 1882, the third church built anywhere in Kitsap County. It was a small, slow-growing place, the kind of town that takes decades to become anything at all.

**The Company That Renamed the Town**

What changed Madrone's fortunes — and its name — was a shipbuilding company. In 1902, the Hall Brothers Marine Railway and Shipbuilding Company moved its operation from Port Blakely to the center of Eagle Harbor, and the town grew up fast around it. Company president Henry Knox Hall built himself a twenty-room house on the strength of it, and he had a hand in renaming the settlement after his brother Winslow, who had died before the company made the move. A dead man's name, given to a harbor of madrone trees. That is how a lot of Pacific Northwest towns got named, if you look closely enough.

**A Ship Called the Eagle**

The town's other big story of 1902 was a fire. In 1900, Eagle Harbor residents pooled their money to build a steamer they called the Eagle, to ferry passengers to Seattle. It ran well for two years — the pride of the community — until August 1902, when it burned to the waterline while tied up at the dock. Its captain, B. F. Klunker, went on to found the town's first newspaper, The Beacon. The Florence K took the Eagle's place on the run, and life went on.

**Twelve Votes**

Decades passed. By 1947, Winslow was ready to become official. The vote came down to an election held August 23 of that year: 122 in favor, 110 against. Twelve votes. On September 18, 1947, Winslow incorporated as a fourth-class city of 905 residents, with Herbert Allen as its first mayor and Ray Williamson, George Townsend, Frank Sheppard, Harold Woodman, and William Roberts on the inaugural council. A town that had spent seven decades finding its footing became a city over a margin you could fit in a single classroom. In the half-century that followed, Winslow grew into the hub of Bainbridge Island, its economy resting on coal, shipbuilding, logging, fishing, and produce — the strawberries and grapes that did well in the island's soil.

**The Vote That Undid It**

The name lasted forty-four years. On February 28, 1991, Winslow annexed the rest of the island. In November 1991, with the island already under one city government, voters decided whether to rename the city Bainbridge Island. It passed, 3,193 to 3,057 — a closer margin than you might expect.

The name didn't vanish, though. Walk off the ferry today and you're on Winslow Way, in a downtown still called Winslow. Madrone was the name given up. The one borrowed from a shipbuilder's dead brother outlasted the city government built on top of it.

**If You Go**

- **Winslow neighborhood** — the historic core of Bainbridge Island, centered on Winslow Way, overlooking Eagle Harbor. This is the original townsite, and the name is still in everyday use even though the city by that name was renamed in 1991.
- **Bainbridge Island Ferry Terminal, 270 Olympic Drive SE** — the ferry lands at the east end of Winslow Way.

Another quiet corner of the Northwest, off the beaten path.
